# Lesson 1
# Introduction to Pharmaceutical Compounding

**Pharmaceutical Compounding and Dispensing Course**
Based on John F. Marriott

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

1. ✓ Define pharmaceutical compounding and explain its importance
2. ✓ Distinguish between compounding and manufacturing
3. ✓ Understand the regulatory framework governing compounding
4. ✓ Identify key USP chapters relevant to compounding
5. ✓ Recognize the role of the pharmacist in compounding

---

## What is Pharmaceutical Compounding?

> **Pharmaceutical compounding** is the preparation, mixing, assembling, packaging, or labeling of a drug or device as the result of a pharmacist's prescription drug order or initiative based on the relationship between the pharmacist, patient, and prescriber.

---

## Key Characteristics

- **Individualized medicine**
  - Tailored to specific patient needs

- **Professional responsibility**
  - Requires pharmacist expertise

- **Quality assured**
  - Must meet appropriate standards

- **Patient-specific**
  - Based on valid prescription or practitioner order

---

## Why is Compounding Important?

### Clinical Needs

1. **Customized dosages**
   - Pediatric or geriatric patients requiring specific strengths

2. **Alternative dosage forms**
   - Patients unable to swallow tablets or capsules

3. **Allergen-free formulations**
   - Removing dyes, preservatives, or other allergens

---

## Why Compounding? (continued)

4. **Discontinued medications**
   - Preparing drugs no longer commercially available

5. **Combination preparations**
   - Multiple drugs in one formulation

---

## Real-World Examples

**Example 1: Pediatric Dosing**
- 5 kg infant requiring 2.5 mg of medication
- Only 10 mg tablets available
- **Solution:** Compound appropriate liquid formulation

**Example 2: Allergy Accommodation**
- Patient allergic to lactose
- Needed medication contains lactose filler
- **Solution:** Compound without lactose

---

## Real-World Examples (continued)

**Example 3: Alternative Route**
- Patient with severe nausea/vomiting
- Cannot take oral medications
- **Solution:** Transdermal preparation

**Example 4: Discontinued Drug**
- Medication removed from market
- Patient still needs it
- **Solution:** Compound from bulk ingredients

---

## Compounding vs. Manufacturing

| Aspect | Compounding | Manufacturing |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Scale** | Small batch, patient-specific | Large batch, commercial |
| **Regulation** | State boards, USP | FDA approval |
| **Prescription** | Required | Sold per label |
| **Innovation** | Novel combinations OK | Must prove safety |
| **Distribution** | Direct to patient | Wholesale |

# Regulatory Framework

Understanding the Rules and Standards

---

## United States Pharmacopeia (USP)

The USP provides official standards for pharmaceutical compounding

**Three Key Chapters:**
- **USP <795>** - Nonsterile Preparations
- **USP <797>** - Sterile Preparations
- **USP <800>** - Hazardous Drugs

---

## USP Chapter <795>
### Pharmaceutical Compounding - Nonsterile Preparations

**Key Topics:**
- Standards for nonsterile compounding
- Beyond-use dating
- Quality control requirements
- Documentation standards
- Personnel training
- Facility requirements

---

## USP Chapter <797>
### Pharmaceutical Compounding - Sterile Preparations

**Key Topics:**
- Standards for sterile compounding
- Risk levels (immediate use, low, medium, high)
- Environmental quality and control
- Cleanroom requirements (ISO 5, ISO 7)
- Personnel training and competency
- Beyond-use dating for sterile products

---

## USP Chapter <800>
### Hazardous Drugs - Handling in Healthcare Settings

**Key Topics:**
- Safe handling of hazardous drugs
- Containment strategies
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)
- Environmental monitoring
- Spill management
- Waste disposal

---

## Other Regulatory Bodies

### State Boards of Pharmacy
- License and regulate pharmacy practice
- Inspect compounding facilities
- Investigate complaints
- May have additional requirements beyond USP

### FDA Oversight
- Drug Quality and Security Act (2013)
- Sections 503A and 503B
- Can inspect facilities
- Enforces regulations

# Professional Responsibilities

What Every Compounding Pharmacist Must Do

---

## 1. Ensure Prescription Validity

✓ Valid prescriber-patient relationship exists
✓ Appropriate indication for compounding
✓ Reasonable dosing
✓ No contraindications

**Red flags:**
- Large quantities without clear need
- Compounding commercially available products
- Prescriber unfamiliar with patient

---

## 2. Source Quality Ingredients

✓ **Use USP/NF grade when available**
✓ **Obtain Certificate of Analysis (COA)**
✓ **Verify supplier reliability**
✓ **Maintain proper storage conditions**

**Documentation Required:**
- Ingredient name and grade
- Lot number
- Expiration date
- Supplier information
- COA on file

---

## 3. Follow Proper Procedures

**Required Documentation:**
- Master formulation records
- Compounding records (batch records)
- Standard operating procedures (SOPs)
- Equipment maintenance logs

**Each compounding session must be documented!**

---

## 4. Perform Quality Control

**Minimum QC for all preparations:**
- ✓ Visual inspection
- ✓ Weight/volume verification
- ✓ Labeling check

**Additional QC when appropriate:**
- pH testing
- Sterility testing (sterile products)
- Potency testing (when required)
- Endotoxin testing (sterile products)

---

## 5. Provide Proper Labeling

**Required Label Information:**
- Patient name
- Prescription number
- Directions for use
- Date compounded
- Beyond-use date
- Compounding pharmacist name/initials
- Storage requirements
- Appropriate warnings

**Auxiliary labels as needed!**

---

## 6. Maintain Documentation

**Keep records of:**
- All compounding activities (batch records)
- Quality control results
- Ingredient sources and lot numbers
- Equipment calibration and maintenance
- Adverse event reports
- Personnel training

**Retention:** Minimum 3-5 years (check state requirements)

# Types of Compounding

From Simple to Complex

---

## Simple Compounding

**Characteristics:**
- Reconstitution of powders
- Mixing commercially available products
- Basic ointment preparations
- Minimal calculations

**Example:**
Mixing hydrocortisone 1% cream with moisturizing cream to make 0.5% strength

---

## Moderate Compounding

**Characteristics:**
- Calculations required
- Special techniques needed
- Multiple ingredients
- Quality control important

**Example:**
Preparing a suspension from tablets using a suspending vehicle
- Requires crushing, levigation
- Need to calculate number of tablets
- Must achieve uniform distribution

---

## Complex Compounding

**Characteristics:**
- Difficult formulations
- Sterile preparations
- Hazardous drugs
- Extensive quality control
- Advanced techniques

**Example:**
Preparing a sterile ophthalmic solution with preservatives
- Requires aseptic technique
- pH adjustment
- Tonicity considerations
- Sterility testing

---

# Essential Equipment and Facilities

The Tools of the Trade

---

## Basic Equipment - Weighing

**Class A Prescription Balance**
- Sensitivity: 6 mg
- For weights > 120 mg
- Requires careful technique

**Electronic Balance**
- Greater sensitivity (0.001 g - 0.01 g)
- Easier to use
- Must be calibrated regularly
- For most compounding work

---

## Basic Equipment - Mixing

**Mortar and Pestle**
- Glass (for liquids)
- Wedgwood (general use)
- Porcelain (heavy-duty)

**Spatulas**
- Stainless steel
- Rubber/plastic (for corrosive substances)
- Various sizes

---

## Basic Equipment - Measuring

**Graduated Cylinders**
- Various sizes (10 mL to 1000 mL)
- Read at meniscus
- More accurate than beakers

**Beakers**
- For mixing, not accurate measuring
- Various sizes

**Pipettes and Syringes**
- For small, accurate volumes

---

## Specialized Equipment

- Ointment slab and pill tile
- Capsule filling machine
- Suppository molds
- Hot plate or water bath
- Homogenizer or mixer
- pH meter
- Sieves (for particle size)

---

## Facility Requirements

**Dedicated Compounding Area:**
✓ Proper ventilation
✓ Adequate lighting
✓ Clean, sanitizable surfaces
✓ Temperature and humidity control
✓ Separate from dispensing area

**For Hazardous Drugs:**
✓ Containment primary engineering control
✓ Negative pressure room

**For Sterile Compounding:**
✓ Cleanroom (ISO 5 environment)
✓ Buffer room (ISO 7)
✓ Ante area

# Safety Considerations

Protecting Yourself and Your Patients

---

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

**Minimum for nonsterile compounding:**
- Lab coat or gown
- Gloves (nitrile recommended)
- Safety glasses

**For sterile compounding (add):**
- Hair covering
- Shoe covers
- Mask

**For hazardous drugs (add):**
- Respirator (when required)
- Double gloving
- Chemotherapy gown

---

## Hygiene Requirements

**Before Compounding:**
✓ Hand washing (at least 30 seconds)
✓ Remove jewelry
✓ Tie back long hair
✓ No artificial nails
✓ Cover cuts/abrasions

**During Compounding:**
✗ No eating or drinking
✗ No cell phone use
✗ Minimize talking
✓ Maintain clean workspace

---

## Environmental Controls

**Required:**
- Regular cleaning and disinfection
- Proper waste disposal
- Spill kits readily available
- Emergency eyewash station
- Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS/SDS) accessible
- Fire extinguisher nearby

**Documentation:**
- Cleaning logs
- Temperature/humidity monitoring
- Equipment maintenance records

# Practice Scenario

Applying What You've Learned

---

## Case Study

**Scenario:**
A pediatrician calls with a prescription for a 2-year-old child (12 kg) who cannot swallow tablets. The medication needed is only available as 25 mg tablets. The prescribed dose is 15 mg twice daily for 10 days.

**Questions:**
1. Is this appropriate for compounding? Why?
2. What dosage form would you recommend?
3. What USP chapter applies?
4. What key information must you document?

---

## Case Study - Answers

1. **Appropriate?** YES
   - Commercial product available but wrong dosage form
   - Patient has legitimate need (cannot swallow tablets)
   - Pediatric patient requiring liquid formulation

2. **Recommended dosage form:**
   - Oral suspension (most appropriate for 2-year-old)

3. **USP Chapter:**
   - <795> (Nonsterile Compounding)

4. **Documentation needed:**
   - Prescription details, calculation verification
   - Source of tablets (lot number, expiration)
   - Compounding procedure followed
   - Quality control results, beyond-use date
