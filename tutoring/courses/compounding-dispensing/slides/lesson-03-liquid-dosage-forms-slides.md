# Overview of Liquid Dosage Forms

Why Liquids Matter

---

## Advantages of Liquid Dosage Forms

✅ **Ease of administration**
- Especially for children and elderly
- No swallowing difficulties

✅ **Dose flexibility**
- Easy to adjust dosage precisely
- Ideal for weight-based dosing

✅ **Rapid absorption**
- Faster onset than solid dosage forms
- Drug already dissolved/dispersed

✅ **Alternative for dysphagia**
- Perfect for patients unable to swallow solids

---

## Disadvantages of Liquid Dosage Forms

❌ **Stability concerns**
- Generally shorter shelf life than solids
- Chemical degradation in water

❌ **Bulky**
- Less convenient to carry
- Requires more storage space

❌ **Taste challenges**
- Many drugs taste bad
- May require flavoring

❌ **Accuracy concerns**
- Measurement errors possible
- Need proper measuring devices

# Solutions

Homogeneous Mixtures at the Molecular Level

---

## What is a Solution?

**Definition:**
A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances at the molecular level.

**Components:**
1. **Solute** - The substance being dissolved (drug)
2. **Solvent** - The substance doing the dissolving (usually water)
3. **Solution** - The resulting homogeneous mixture

**Key characteristic:** Molecularly dispersed (cannot see individual particles)

---

## Types of Solutions - By Solvent

### Aqueous Solutions
- Water is the solvent
- Most common in pharmacy
- Examples: Simple syrup, irrigation solutions

### Alcoholic Solutions
- Alcohol is the solvent
- Examples: Tinctures, spirits

### Hydroalcoholic Solutions
- Mixture of water and alcohol
- Examples: Elixirs, mouthwashes

---

## Types of Solutions (continued)

### Non-aqueous Solutions
- Other solvents (oils, propylene glycol, glycerin)
- Examples: Oil-based ear drops

**Special Types:**
- **Syrups** - High sugar content (≥45% sucrose)
- **Elixirs** - Sweetened hydroalcoholic solutions
- **Tinctures** - Alcoholic extracts
- **Spirits** - Alcoholic solutions of volatile substances

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# Principles of Solubility

Understanding Why Things Dissolve

---

## "Like Dissolves Like"

**Golden Rule of Solubility:**

**Polar solutes** dissolve in **polar solvents**
- Example: Salt (polar) in water (polar) ✓

**Non-polar solutes** dissolve in **non-polar solvents**
- Example: Oil (non-polar) in mineral oil (non-polar) ✓

**Polar and non-polar DON'T mix**
- Example: Oil and water ✗

---

## Factors Affecting Solubility

### 1. Temperature
- **Most solids:** Solubility ↑ as temperature ↑
- **Gases:** Solubility ↓ as temperature ↑
- **Practical:** Heat may be used to aid dissolution

### 2. pH
- **Weak acids:** More soluble in alkaline pH
- **Weak bases:** More soluble in acidic pH
- **Practical:** May need pH adjustment

---

## Factors Affecting Solubility (continued)

### 3. Particle Size
- Smaller particles dissolve faster
- Greater surface area for dissolution
- **Practical:** May need to reduce particle size (comminution)

### 4. Agitation
- Stirring increases dissolution rate
- Brings fresh solvent to solute surface
- **Practical:** Mix well during preparation

### 5. Nature of Solute and Solvent
- Chemical structure matters
- "Like dissolves like" principle

---

# Compounding Pharmaceutical Solutions

Step-by-Step Process

---

## General Procedure for Solutions

1. **Calculate quantities** of all ingredients
2. **Gather and verify** all materials and equipment
3. **Reduce particle size** if needed (powder drugs in mortar)
4. **Dissolve solute** in portion of solvent with mixing
5. **Add additional ingredients** (preservatives, buffers, flavorings)
6. **Adjust pH** if necessary
7. **Bring to final volume** with solvent (qs to final volume)
8. **Mix thoroughly**
9. **Quality control** (visual inspection, pH, clarity)
10. **Package and label** appropriately

---

## Example: Normal Saline Preparation

**Prescription:**
Sodium Chloride Solution 0.9% (Normal Saline)
Make 500 mL

**Calculation:**
```
500 mL × (0.9 g NaCl / 100 mL) = 4.5 g NaCl
```

---

## Normal Saline - Procedure

**Step-by-step:**
1. Weigh 4.5 g of sodium chloride USP
2. Transfer to beaker
3. Add approximately 400 mL purified water
4. Stir until completely dissolved
5. Transfer to graduated cylinder
6. Add purified water qs to 500 mL
7. Mix well
8. Check clarity and pH
9. Package in appropriate bottle
10. Label with instructions and beyond-use date

---

## Order of Mixing

**General rule - Add ingredients in this order:**

1. Substances requiring heat (if any)
2. Preservatives
3. Active ingredients
4. Buffers and pH adjusters
5. Sweeteners
6. Flavorings and colorings (LAST)

**Why this order?**
- Ensures stability
- Prevents incompatibilities
- Maintains effectiveness

# Suspensions

Dispersed but Not Dissolved

---

## What is a Suspension?

**Definition:**
A suspension is a dispersion of finely divided, undissolved drug particles in a liquid vehicle.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Heterogeneous** - Not uniform at molecular level
- **Unstable** - Particles tend to settle or aggregate
- **Require shaking** - "Shake Well" label REQUIRED
- **Opaque appearance** - Due to suspended particles

---

## Solutions vs. Suspensions

| Feature | Solution | Suspension |
|---------|----------|------------|
| **Homogeneity** | Homogeneous | Heterogeneous |
| **Particle size** | Molecular | Larger particles |
| **Appearance** | Clear | Opaque/cloudy |
| **Stability** | Stable | Tend to settle |
| **Shaking** | Not needed | REQUIRED |
| **Preparation** | Simpler | More complex |

---

## When to Use Suspensions

### 1. Drug is insoluble
- Cannot dissolve in available solvents

### 2. Improved stability
- Some drugs more stable as suspension
- Avoid degradation in solution

### 3. Improved taste
- Insoluble form may taste better than dissolved drug

### 4. Controlled release
- Slower dissolution = prolonged action

### 5. High drug concentration needed
- Can achieve higher concentrations than solutions

---

# Theory of Suspensions

Understanding Particle Behavior

---

## Stokes' Law - Settling Rate

**Particles in suspension settle according to:**

```
v = [d² × (ρₚ - ρₘ) × g] / (18 × η)
```

**Where:**
- v = settling velocity
- d = particle diameter
- ρₚ = particle density
- ρₘ = medium density
- η = viscosity of medium

---

## Practical Implications of Stokes' Law

### To SLOW settling:

✓ **Reduce particle size (d)**
- Comminution, trituration
- Smaller particles settle slower

✓ **Increase viscosity (η)**
- Add suspending agents
- Makes medium "thicker"

✓ **Match densities (ρₚ ≈ ρₘ)**
- Adjust vehicle density
- Less difference = slower settling

---

## Suspending Agents

**Purpose:** Increase viscosity and slow particle settling

### Natural Polymers:
- **Acacia (Gum Arabic)** - 10-20%
- **Tragacanth** - 1-2%
- **Sodium alginate** - 1-5%

### Semisynthetic Cellulose Derivatives:
- **Methylcellulose (MC)** - 1-2%
- **Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC)** - 0.5-2%
- **Hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC)** - 1-5%

---

## Suspending Agents (continued)

### Synthetic Polymers:
- **Carbomer (Carbopol)** - 0.5-2%
- **Povidone (PVP)** - 2-10%

### Clays:
- **Bentonite** - 2-5% (requires activation with water)
- **Veegum** - 2-5%

**Selection depends on:**
- Drug compatibility
- Desired viscosity
- pH requirements
- Cost

---

# Preparing Suspensions

Techniques for Success

---

## Method 1: Levigation

**Use when:** Starting with a powder

**Levigating agent:** Small amount of liquid to make paste
- Glycerin (for aqueous vehicles)
- Mineral oil (for oil-based vehicles)
- Propylene glycol
- Small amount of final vehicle

**Purpose:** Create smooth, uniform paste before adding vehicle

---

## Levigation Procedure

1. Place powder in mortar
2. Add **small amount** of levigating agent (glycerin, propylene glycol)
3. **Triturate** to form smooth paste (no lumps!)
4. Add vehicle in **geometric portions** with continued trituration
5. Transfer to graduate
6. Rinse mortar with vehicle and add to graduate
7. Qs to final volume with vehicle
8. Package and label
9. **Add "Shake Well" label**

**Critical:** The initial paste must be completely smooth!

---

## Method 2: From Tablets or Capsules

**Use when:** Starting with commercial dosage form

**Procedure:**
1. Count required number of tablets/capsules
2. Crush tablets or empty capsules in mortar
3. Add small amount of vehicle to form paste
4. Triturate thoroughly (remove all lumps)
5. Add vehicle in portions with mixing
6. Transfer to graduate
7. Rinse mortar and add to graduate
8. Qs to final volume
9. Package and label ("Shake Well")

**Important:** Verify no extended-release formulation!

---

## Flocculated vs. Deflocculated Suspensions

### Deflocculated (Structured):
- Individual particles
- Slow settling
- Forms **tight sediment** (caking problem)
- Difficult to redisperse
- Elegant appearance

### Flocculated:
- Clumped particles
- Faster settling
- Forms **loose sediment**
- Easy to redisperse
- Less elegant but more practical

**Goal:** Controlled flocculation for easy redispersion

---

## Wetting Agents

**For:** Hydrophobic drugs that don't mix well with water

**Purpose:** Reduce surface tension, allow water to penetrate powder

**Examples:**
- Polysorbate 80
- Sodium lauryl sulfate
- Polyethylene glycol

**Procedure:**
1. Mix powder with wetting agent
2. Allow to stand (wet thoroughly)
3. Add vehicle with mixing

---

# Additives in Liquid Preparations

Ensuring Quality and Stability

---

## Preservatives

**Purpose:** Protect against microbial contamination

**Common preservatives:**
- **Methylparaben** 0.05-0.25% (molds/yeasts)
- **Propylparaben** 0.01-0.06% (bacteria)
- **Combination:** methylparaben 0.18% + propylparaben 0.02%
- **Benzoic acid** 0.1-0.5%
- **Sorbic acid** 0.05-0.2%
- **Benzalkonium chloride** 0.002-0.02%

---

## When Preservatives NOT Needed

❌ **High alcohol content** (>15-20%)
❌ **High sugar content** (syrups)
❌ **For immediate use** (single dose)
❌ **Intrinsically antimicrobial** (strong acids/bases)

✅ **When preservatives ARE needed:**
- Multi-dose aqueous preparations
- Storage beyond immediate use
- Risk of contamination

---

## Antioxidants

**Purpose:** Prevent oxidative degradation

**Common antioxidants:**
- **Ascorbic acid** 0.05-0.1%
- **Sodium bisulfite** 0.1-0.15%
- **Sodium metabisulfite** 0.1-1%
- **BHA** 0.001-0.02%
- **BHT** 0.001-0.02%

**Use when:** Drug susceptible to oxidation

---

## Buffers

**Purpose:** Maintain stable pH

**Common buffer systems:**
- **Citrate buffer** (pH 3-6)
- **Acetate buffer** (pH 4-6)
- **Phosphate buffer** (pH 6-8)
- **Carbonate buffer** (pH 9-11)

**Important for:**
- Drug stability
- Patient comfort
- Preventing degradation

---

## Sweeteners

### Nutritive (provide calories):
- **Sucrose** (most common in syrups)
- **Sorbitol** 30-70% (for diabetics)
- **Glycerin** 20-50%

### Non-nutritive (no calories):
- **Saccharin sodium** 0.02-0.5%
- **Aspartame** (not for PKU patients)
- **Sucralose** 0.016-0.03%

**Purpose:** Improve palatability, especially for pediatrics

---

## Flavorings

**Principles:**
- Match flavor to drug taste
- **Salty/bitter** → fruit flavors (cherry, raspberry, orange)
- **Sour** → citrus or berry flavors
- **Oil-based drugs** → spice flavors (mint, cinnamon)

**Types:**
- Natural extracts
- Synthetic flavors
- Essential oils

**Concentration:** Usually 0.1-0.5%

**Add last** to avoid loss of volatile components

---

# Quality Control for Liquid Preparations

Ensuring Safety and Efficacy

---

## Visual Inspection - Solutions

**Solutions should be:**
✓ **Clear** (may shine light through)
✓ **Free from particles**
✓ **Uniform color**
✓ **No cloudiness**

**Check against:**
- Black background (see light particles)
- White background (see dark particles)
- Good lighting essential

---

## Visual Inspection - Suspensions

**Suspensions should show:**
✓ **Uniform appearance** after shaking
✓ **No caking** on bottom
✓ **Appropriate viscosity** (not too thick/thin)
✓ **No separation** of vehicle
✓ **Homogeneous** when shaken

**Red flags:**
- Hard cake at bottom
- Crystal formation
- Vehicle separation
- Color change

---

## pH Testing

**Procedure:**
- Use calibrated pH meter or pH paper
- Document pH value
- Should be within expected range for formulation

**Important for:**
- Drug stability
- Patient comfort (especially for eye drops, nasal solutions)
- Preventing degradation
- Regulatory compliance

**Document:** Record pH in compounding record

---

## Volume Check

**Procedure:**
- Use graduated cylinder
- Verify final volume is correct
- Read at meniscus (eye level)

**Important because:**
- Ensures accurate dosing
- Verifies proper preparation
- Required for documentation

**Acceptable range:** Usually ±2% of target volume

---

## Beyond-Use Dating

**Per USP <795> for water-containing preparations:**

### **14 days** when stored at controlled cold temperature (2-8°C)

**May be longer if:**
- Stability data available
- Published formulations
- Sterility maintained (for sterile products)

**Must consider:**
- Drug stability
- Preservative efficacy
- Container/closure
- Storage conditions

# Practical Examples

Let's Compound!

---

## Example 1: Oral Antibiotic Suspension

**Prescription:**
```
Amoxicillin 50 mg/mL suspension
Make 100 mL
Sig: Take 5 mL twice daily
```

**Available:** Amoxicillin 500 mg capsules

---

## Example 1: Calculations

**Step 1: Calculate total drug needed**
```
100 mL × 50 mg/mL = 5000 mg amoxicillin
```

**Step 2: Calculate number of capsules**
```
5000 mg ÷ 500 mg/capsule = 10 capsules
```

**Step 3: Select vehicle**
- Ora-Plus 50 mL (suspending agent)
- Ora-Sweet SF 50 mL (sweetener, sugar-free)

---

## Example 1: Procedure

1. Empty 10 amoxicillin capsules into mortar
2. Add small amount of Ora-Plus to form paste
3. Triturate well (2 minutes, until smooth)
4. Add Ora-Plus in portions with mixing
5. Transfer to graduate
6. Rinse mortar with Ora-Sweet SF
7. Qs to 100 mL with Ora-Sweet SF
8. Package in amber bottle
9. Label: "**Shake well before use. Keep refrigerated. Discard after 14 days.**"

---

## Example 1: Quality Control

**Visual inspection:**
- White to off-white suspension ✓
- Uniform after shaking ✓
- No large clumps ✓

**Volume check:**
- 100 mL ✓

**Labeling:**
- Patient name, directions, BUD ✓
- "Shake Well" label ✓
- "Refrigerate" label ✓

**Beyond-use date:** 14 days refrigerated

---

## Example 2: Flavored Solution

**Prescription:**
```
Potassium Chloride 10% solution, flavored
Make 120 mL
```

**Formulation:**
- Potassium chloride: 12 g
- Cherry flavor: 0.2 mL
- Sucralose: 0.05 g
- Purified water qs to 120 mL

---

## Example 2: Procedure

1. Weigh 12 g potassium chloride
2. Dissolve in approximately 100 mL water
3. Add sucralose and dissolve
4. Add cherry flavor
5. Qs to 120 mL with water
6. Mix thoroughly
7. Check pH (should be approximately 5-7)
8. Package and label
9. BUD: 14 days refrigerated

# Packaging Considerations

The Right Container Matters

---

## Container Selection - Solutions

**For solutions:**
- Tight, light-resistant (amber) bottles if light-sensitive
- Dropper bottles for ophthalmic/otic solutions
- Oral syringes for accurate pediatric dosing
- Clear bottles if drug not light-sensitive

**Size:** Slightly larger than final volume (allow headspace)

**Material:** Glass or appropriate plastic (HDPE, PET)

---

## Container Selection - Suspensions

**For suspensions:**
- **Wide-mouth** bottles (easier to shake and pour)
- Amber bottles if light-sensitive
- Adequate headspace for shaking
- Calibrated cap or measuring device

**Important:** Must be able to shake well!

---

## Closure Types

### Child-Resistant Closures (CRC)
- Required for most prescriptions
- Federal regulation (Poison Prevention Act)

### Non-Child-Resistant
- If requested by patient
- If patient unable to use CRC
- Institutional use

### Special Closures
- Dropper caps for drops
- Oral dosing syringes for accurate measurement

# Key Takeaways

What You Must Remember

---

## Summary - Part 1

1. **Solutions = Homogeneous; Suspensions = Heterogeneous**
   - Solutions: molecularly dispersed
   - Suspensions: visible particles

2. **"Like Dissolves Like"**
   - Consider polarity when selecting solvents
   - Match solute and solvent properties

3. **Suspensions Require Suspending Agents**
   - Slow particle settling
   - Always need "Shake Well" labels

---

## Summary - Part 2

4. **Levigation is Key**
   - Technique for smooth suspensions from powders
   - Create paste before adding vehicle

5. **Preservatives Needed**
   - For water-containing preparations
   - Unless exempted (high alcohol, high sugar, immediate use)

6. **Beyond-Use Dating**
   - 14 days refrigerated for water-containing preparations (USP <795>)
   - Document storage requirements

---

## Summary - Part 3

7. **Quality Control**
   - Visual inspection (clarity for solutions, uniformity for suspensions)
   - pH testing
   - Volume verification

8. **Proper Flavoring**
   - Improves patient compliance
   - Especially important for pediatric patients
   - Add last to preserve volatile components

# Review Questions

---

## Questions 1-3

1. **What is the difference between a solution and a suspension?**

2. **According to Stokes' Law, how can you slow the settling of particles in a suspension?**

3. **What is the purpose of a levigating agent?**

---

## Answers 1-3

1. **Solution vs Suspension:**
   - Solution: Homogeneous, molecularly dispersed, clear
   - Suspension: Heterogeneous, visible particles, opaque, requires shaking

2. **Slow settling by:**
   - Reduce particle size
   - Increase viscosity (add suspending agents)
   - Match particle and vehicle densities

3. **Levigating agent:**
   - Creates smooth paste from powder
   - Ensures uniform distribution
   - Prevents clumping before adding vehicle

---

## Questions 4-6

4. **Why do suspensions require a "Shake Well" label?**

5. **What is the maximum beyond-use date for a water-containing compounded preparation stored in the refrigerator per USP <795>?**

6. **Name three suspending agents and their typical concentrations.**

---

## Answers 4-6

4. **"Shake Well" because:**
   - Particles settle over time
   - Need to redistribute before use
   - Ensures uniform dose

5. **Beyond-use date:**
   - 14 days when stored refrigerated (2-8°C)

6. **Suspending agents:**
   - Methylcellulose 1-2%
   - CMC (carboxymethylcellulose) 0.5-2%
   - Carbomer 0.5-2%
