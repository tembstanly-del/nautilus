# Pharmaceutical Compounding and Dispensing Course

## Course Overview

This course provides comprehensive training in pharmaceutical compounding and dispensing based on principles from:
- **"Pharmaceutical Compounding and Dispensing" by John F. Marriott**
- **"Pharmaceutical Calculations: A Conceptual Approach"**

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Understand the principles and practices of pharmaceutical compounding
2. Perform pharmaceutical calculations accurately using conceptual approaches
3. Select appropriate equipment and techniques for different dosage forms
4. Apply quality control measures to compounded preparations
5. Follow proper documentation and regulatory requirements
6. Ensure patient safety through proper labeling and counseling
7. Demonstrate competency in aseptic technique when required

## Course Structure

### Module 1: Introduction to Pharmaceutical Compounding
- History and importance of compounding
- Legal and regulatory framework
- USP chapters 795 and 797
- Equipment and facilities

### Module 2: Pharmaceutical Calculations
- Fundamental calculation concepts
- Dosage calculations
- Concentration and dilution
- Percentage strength and ratio strength
- Alligation methods

### Module 3: Dosage Forms - Liquids
- Solutions
- Suspensions
- Emulsions
- Syrups and elixirs

### Module 4: Dosage Forms - Semi-solids
- Ointments
- Creams
- Gels
- Pastes

### Module 5: Dosage Forms - Solids
- Powders and granules
- Capsules
- Tablets (limited compounding)
- Suppositories and pessaries

### Module 6: Sterile Compounding
- Aseptic technique
- Cleanroom requirements
- Sterility testing
- Beyond-use dating for sterile preparations

### Module 7: Quality Assurance
- Quality control testing
- Documentation requirements
- Stability considerations
- Beyond-use dating

### Module 8: Dispensing and Patient Care
- Prescription interpretation
- Labeling requirements
- Patient counseling
- Medication safety

## Assessment Methods

- Weekly quizzes on calculations
- Practical demonstrations
- Compounding laboratory exercises
- Written examinations
- Case study analyses

## Prerequisites

- Basic chemistry knowledge
- Understanding of pharmaceutical sciences
- Basic mathematics skills

## Duration

8 weeks (60 hours total)
- Lectures: 24 hours
- Laboratory practice: 24 hours
- Self-study: 12 hours

## Required Materials

- Calculator
- Laboratory coat and personal protective equipment
- Access to compounding laboratory
- Course textbooks

## Grading

- Quizzes: 20%
- Laboratory work: 30%
- Midterm examination: 20%
- Final examination: 20%
- Case studies: 10%
