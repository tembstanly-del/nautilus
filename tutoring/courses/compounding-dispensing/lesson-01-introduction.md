# Lesson 1: Introduction to Pharmaceutical Compounding

## Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Define pharmaceutical compounding and explain its importance
2. Distinguish between compounding and manufacturing
3. Understand the regulatory framework governing compounding
4. Identify key USP chapters relevant to compounding
5. Recognize the role of the pharmacist in compounding

## What is Pharmaceutical Compounding?

**Pharmaceutical compounding** is the preparation, mixing, assembling, packaging, or labeling of a drug or device as the result of a pharmacist's prescription drug order or initiative based on the relationship between the pharmacist, patient, and prescriber in the course of professional practice.

### Key Characteristics

- **Individualized medicine**: Tailored to specific patient needs
- **Professional responsibility**: Requires pharmacist expertise
- **Quality assured**: Must meet appropriate standards
- **Patient-specific**: Based on valid prescription or practitioner order

## Why is Compounding Important?

### Clinical Needs
1. **Customized dosages**: Pediatric or geriatric patients requiring specific strengths
2. **Alternative dosage forms**: Patients unable to swallow tablets or capsules
3. **Allergen-free formulations**: Removing dyes, preservatives, or other allergens
4. **Discontinued medications**: Preparing drugs no longer commercially available
5. **Combination preparations**: Multiple drugs in one formulation

### Examples of Compounding Needs

- A 5 kg infant requiring 2.5 mg of medication when only 10 mg tablets are available
- A patient allergic to lactose needing medication without lactose filler
- Transdermal preparations for patients with nausea/vomiting
- Veterinary medications in flavored suspensions
- Hormone replacement therapy in customized strengths

## Compounding vs. Manufacturing

| Aspect | Compounding | Manufacturing |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Scale | Small batch, patient-specific | Large batch, commercial |
| Regulation | State boards, USP standards | FDA approval required |
| Prescription | Requires valid prescription | Sold per approved labeling |
| Innovation | Can create novel combinations | Must prove safety/efficacy |
| Distribution | Direct to patient | Wholesale distribution |

## Regulatory Framework

### United States Pharmacopeia (USP)

The USP provides standards for compounding through several key chapters:

#### **USP Chapter <795>: Pharmaceutical Compounding - Nonsterile Preparations**
- Standards for nonsterile compounding
- Beyond-use dating
- Quality control requirements
- Documentation

#### **USP Chapter <797>: Pharmaceutical Compounding - Sterile Preparations**
- Standards for sterile compounding
- Risk levels (immediate use, low, medium, high risk)
- Environmental quality and control
- Personnel training and competency
- Beyond-use dating for sterile preparations

#### **USP Chapter <800>: Hazardous Drugs - Handling in Healthcare Settings**
- Safe handling of hazardous drugs
- Containment strategies
- Personal protective equipment
- Environmental monitoring

### State Boards of Pharmacy

- License and regulate pharmacy practice
- Inspect compounding facilities
- Investigate complaints
- May have additional requirements beyond USP

### FDA Oversight

- Drug Quality and Security Act (DQSA) of 2013
- Sections 503A (traditional compounding) and 503B (outsourcing facilities)
- Can inspect facilities
- Enforces regulations against large-scale manufacturing disguised as compounding

## Professional Responsibilities

### The Compounding Pharmacist Must:

1. **Ensure prescription validity**
   - Valid prescriber-patient relationship
   - Appropriate indication
   - Reasonable dosing

2. **Source quality ingredients**
   - USP/NF grade when available
   - Certificate of analysis (COA)
   - Proper storage conditions

3. **Follow proper procedures**
   - Master formulation records
   - Compounding records
   - Standard operating procedures (SOPs)

4. **Perform quality control**
   - Visual inspection
   - Weight/volume verification
   - pH testing when appropriate
   - Potency testing when required

5. **Provide proper labeling**
   - All required information
   - Clear directions
   - Appropriate warnings
   - Beyond-use date

6. **Maintain documentation**
   - Batch records
   - Quality control results
   - Adverse event reporting
   - Equipment maintenance logs

## Types of Compounding

### 1. Simple Compounding
- Reconstitution of powders
- Mixing two or more commercially available products
- Basic ointment preparations
- **Example**: Mixing hydrocortisone cream with moisturizer

### 2. Moderate Compounding
- Calculations required
- Special techniques needed
- Multiple ingredients
- **Example**: Preparing a suspension from tablets with suspending vehicle

### 3. Complex Compounding
- Difficult formulations
- Sterile preparations
- Hazardous drugs
- Extensive quality control
- **Example**: Preparing a sterile ophthalmic solution with preservatives

## Essential Equipment and Facilities

### Basic Equipment
- **Class A prescription balance** (sensitivity: 6 mg)
- **Electronic balance** (calibrated regularly)
- Mortar and pestle (glass, wedgwood, porcelain)
- Spatulas (stainless steel, rubber, plastic)
- Graduated cylinders (various sizes)
- Beakers
- Stirring rods
- Funnels and filters

### Specialized Equipment
- Ointment slab and pill tile
- Capsule machine
- Suppository molds
- Hot plate or water bath
- Homogenizer or mixer
- pH meter

### Facility Requirements
- Dedicated compounding area
- Proper ventilation
- Adequate lighting
- Clean, sanitizable surfaces
- Temperature and humidity control
- Separate area for hazardous drug handling
- Cleanroom for sterile compounding (ISO 5 environment)

## Safety Considerations

### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- Lab coat or gown
- Gloves (nitrile recommended)
- Safety glasses
- Hair covering
- Shoe covers (in cleanrooms)
- Respirator (for hazardous drugs)

### Hygiene Requirements
- Hand washing before compounding
- No jewelry
- Tied back hair
- No artificial nails
- No eating or drinking in compounding area

### Environmental Controls
- Regular cleaning and disinfection
- Proper waste disposal
- Spill kits available
- Emergency eyewash station
- Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) accessible

## Key Takeaways

1. Compounding is the preparation of customized medications for specific patient needs
2. Pharmacists have professional responsibility to ensure quality and safety
3. USP chapters provide standards for compounding practice
4. Proper equipment, facilities, and techniques are essential
5. Documentation and quality control are critical components
6. Patient safety is the primary concern in all compounding activities
